“Signs You Used AI”
===================

Is AI Supposed to Gather Dust?
------------------------------

There is a genre of content I increasingly find amusing:

**“Signs you used AI.”**

The weird phrasing.
The em dash.
The suspiciously polished paragraphs.
The “delve.”
The fact that someone used **_z_** instead of **_s_.** 😂

At some point, though, I have to ask:

**Is AI supposed to gather dust?**

AI has become rather quite ubiquitous.

It is being integrated into search, software development, customer service, marketing, research, education, analytics, design, administration and practically every other process where information goes in and something useful needs to come out.

So, yes, you should take advantage of AI.

And if you’re _not_ using it to streamline your processes, build workflows, create templates, automate repetitive tasks and accelerate the work you already do, then…

**what are you doing?**

The conversation around AI sometimes gets stuck at the most superficial level.

_*eyeroll*_

People are still trying to identify whether something was written by AI instead of asking the more interesting question:

**Was AI used well?**

Those are two very different questions.

_I don’t particularly care if someone used AI to draft an email._

**I care whether the email makes sense.**

_I don’t care if someone used AI to brainstorm a strategy._

**I care whether the strategy is actually good.**

_I don’t care if someone used AI to write code._

**I care whether the code works, is maintainable and doesn’t introduce a spectacular security problem.**

The tool isn’t the interesting part.

**The process is.**

And this is where I think AI becomes genuinely valuable.

Don’t just ask AI to write things for you.

Build a repeatable process around it.

Create a prompt you can reuse.

Create a template.

Create a workflow.

Connect multiple steps.

Automate the boring parts.

Feed it the context it actually needs.

Review the output.

Improve the process.

Then do it again.

That’s not “cheating.”

That’s **process optimisation**.

We’ve spent decades building software specifically so humans don’t have to manually perform repetitive tasks. AI is simply expanding the category of tasks that can be streamlined.

And there is another reason I think you should use these tools while you can.

**I have absolutely no idea what AI is going to cost in five or ten years.**

That is probably my primary, biggest concern with AI.

Right now, regular people can access increasingly capable models for relatively little money, sometimes for free. But technology has a funny habit of changing its pricing, availability and business models once it becomes infrastructure.

Maybe AI gets dramatically cheaper.

Maybe it becomes mostly commoditised.

Maybe the best capabilities become expensive.

Maybe the free tier becomes increasingly restrictive.

Who knows?

So while these tools are accessible, **learn how to use them.**

Don’t just learn how to prompt.

Learn how to incorporate them into your work.

Learn what should be automated and what shouldn’t.

Learn how to evaluate their output.

Learn how to build workflows around them.

Learn how to make yourself faster.

Because if AI eventually becomes expensive, you’ll at least have something valuable:

**the knowledge of what to do with it.**

And then there is, for me, only one real “sign” that AI was used.

**You didn’t bother to localise the output.**

You took whatever the model produced, copied it, pasted it and published it without considering your audience.

That’s where AI becomes painfully obvious.

Not because AI was involved.

Because **you weren’t.**

The best AI-assisted work still sounds like it belongs to the person, company, community or market it was created for.

AI can generate the first version.

You provide the context.

You provide the judgement.

You provide the cultural understanding.

You provide the final edit.

That’s the difference between **using AI** and **outsourcing your brain to AI.**

And yes, I hypocritically use _z_… *coughs* sometimes, you know that I have simply accepted [**z supremacy in the tech arena**](https://medium.com/accepting-z-supremacy-80929e40e929)**.**

But that’s about it.

AI is here.

You can spend your time obsessing over whether someone used it, or you can figure out how to use it better than the person sitting next to you.

**Use it before you lose it.**
