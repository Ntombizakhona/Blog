Chronicles of a Polymath
========================

The Agentic IDE That Turned Me Into A Renaissance Developer
-----------------------------------------------------------

So, a few months ago, a freshly released Agentic IDE turned me into a **_Renaissance Developer._**

Not metaphorically.

Not philosophically.

**Literally!**

I ended up learning about hardware, storage recovery, those ugly Windows commands, backups, operating systems, local environments, and the terrifying difference between _“the AI can probably handle this”_ and _“why is my laptop behaving like it has made independent life decisions?”_

And somehow…

After all of that…

**I downloaded the IDE again.**

But let’s start at the beginning.

A Few Months Ago, I Wrote About The Renaissance Developer
---------------------------------------------------------

### A few months ago, I wrote about [The Renaissance Developer](https://medium.com/the-renaissance-developer-9c5fc233ec11).

The central idea was inspired by Werner Vogels’ concept of the modern polymath: the idea that the next generation of developers cannot afford to exist entirely inside one narrow technical silo.

A developer increasingly needs breadth.

Cloud.

AI.

Data.

Security.

Systems thinking.

Communication.

Ownership.

And perhaps most importantly:

**Curiosity.**

I wrote about being an autodidact and how technology increasingly forces us to connect multiple disciplines rather than simply mastering one tool and staying there forever.

At the time, this was largely a philosophical idea.

Then Agentic AI gave me a practical demonstration.

An extremely practical demonstration.

One that involved **screwdrivers.**

The Agentic IDE
---------------

### So, a freshly released (uhm, it actually wasn’t GA yet I think, maybe one day fresh) Agentic IDE came along.

And naturally…

I downloaded it.

Because that is what curious people do.

New technology appears.

You read about it.

You watch people talking about it.

You think:

> **_“Hmm. Let me just see what this thing can do.”_**

And then suddenly you are several hours deep into an experiment wondering whether your computer still belongs entirely to you.

Now, I believe this particular IDE tried to access the **rootiest root folder to ever root**.

I am talking about ROOT root.

The kind of root where you start asking yourself:

> **_“Wait… why does this application need to know that?”_**

And then something else happened.

I believe it attempted to update my laptop.

Again…

**My laptop.**

_The entire laptop._

Not the IDE.

Not a dependency.

Not a Python package.

The actual Windows laptop.

### I Saw The Most Verbose Natural Language System Message

What really caught my attention was seeing one of the most verbose update messages I had ever seen on Windows.

And the style of that message was very much in line with a particular model.

Now, I cannot definitively say exactly what happened internally.

But I can say this:

**I had questions.**

Lots of questions.

When Software Problems Become Hardware Problems
-----------------------------------------------

At some point, I decided that uninstalling the IDE was not enough.

_No._

We had moved beyond that.

I was now in new territory:

> **_“I need to physically remove this thing from my computing environment.”_**

So I manually removed the IDE.

And by manually…

I mean **manually manually**.

As in:

🪛 Grab the screwdrivers.

💻 Open the laptop.

💾 Remove the hard drive.

📦 Put the drive into an enclosure.

🖥️ Connect it to another computer.

⌨️ Use Windows commands to access and back up my files.

🔄 Recover the laptop from scratch.

At this point, I had gone from:

> **_“Let me try this new AI-powered IDE.”_**

to:

> **_Computer Hardware Technician!_**

The Abstraction Layer Disappeared
---------------------------------

This experience made me think about something important.

Modern computing is built on [abstraction.](https://medium.com/@ntombizakhona/agentic-ai-is-the-latest-abstraction-72722c4e5d1d)

Most of the time, that is a wonderful thing.

You write code without thinking about transistors.

You deploy applications without physically touching servers.

You use cloud storage without walking into a data centre.

You install software without thinking about the physical storage device underneath your operating system.

Until something goes wrong.

Then suddenly…

The abstraction layers start disappearing.

One by one.

First:

> **_“The application is behaving strangely.”_**

Then:

> **_“Let me uninstall it.”_**

Then:

> **_“Let me recover the operating system.”_**

Then:

> **_“Let me access the storage directly.”_**

Then:

> **_“Where is my screwdriver?”_**

And suddenly, you are learning things you never planned to learn that day.

AI Expands The Responsibility Surface
-------------------------------------

This is one of the things I think people need to understand about AI.

**AI is incredibly powerful.**

_But as AI systems become more capable, they also interact with more of our computing environments._

They can read files.

Generate code.

Execute commands.

Modify environments.

Install dependencies.

Interact with terminals.

Connect to cloud services.

Operate inside repositories.

And increasingly…

**Take actions.**

The more capability we give a tool, the more important it becomes to understand the environment in which that tool operates.

This is why I do not think the future developer can only know one thing.

You cannot only know:

> **_“How to write Python.”_**

Or:

> **_“How to use an AI model.”_**

Or:

> **_“How to prompt.”_**

Because eventually, something will happen outside that narrow area.

Your code will interact with an operating system.

Your operating system will interact with storage.

Your application will interact with the cloud.

Your AI agent will interact with your local environment.

Your local environment may interact with production infrastructure.

And suddenly…

Everything is connected.

That is systems thinking.

You Need To Know More Than One Layer
------------------------------------

With AI, I increasingly believe developers need awareness across multiple layers.

Not necessarily expert-level knowledge in every single one.

But enough knowledge to understand what is happening.

### Hardware

You should understand that your computer is not magic.

At some point, there is physical hardware underneath everything.

_Storage._

_Memory._

_Processors._

_Motherboards._

_Drives._

And sometimes…

You may need to physically remove something.

Apparently.

### Local Computing

You should understand your local environment.

Where your files live.

What applications are running.

What permissions exist.

What processes are doing.

What gets installed.

What has access to your system.

Because AI agents increasingly operate locally.

And “local” is not an abstract concept.

It is your computer.

Your files.

Your environment.

Your responsibility.

### The Command Line

The command line remains one of the most powerful interfaces in computing.

Graphical interfaces are wonderful.

But when something breaks, the CLI often gives you visibility and control that a graphical interface does not.

The command line lets you inspect.

Move.

Copy.

Recover.

Configure.

Automate.

And troubleshoot.

In my case, it also became part of the backup and recovery process.

Because sometimes the GUI is not enough.

Sometimes you need to talk directly to the machine.

### The Cloud

Then there is the cloud.

Because increasingly, our local machines are only one part of the system.

Applications interact with APIs.

Agents interact with cloud services.

Repositories connect to remote platforms.

AI systems call external models.

Data moves between local and cloud environments.

Infrastructure is provisioned programmatically.

The boundaries are becoming increasingly blurry.

1.  Local.
2.  Cloud.
3.  AI.
4.  CLI.
5.  Hardware.

These are no longer completely separate worlds.

The Autodidact Advantage
------------------------

This is where being an autodidact becomes incredibly valuable.

An autodidact is not someone who magically knows everything.

An autodidact is someone who is willing to encounter something they do not understand…

and then go learn it.

That distinction is important.

I did not wake up that morning thinking:

> **_“Today I would like to remove my laptop’s storage device and perform a recovery operation.”_**

Absolutely not.

But the problem appeared.

And suddenly there was something to learn.

That is the autodidact mindset.

You don’t wait for a curriculum to tell you:

> **_“Today you are allowed to learn hardware.”_**

You don’t wait for a job description to tell you:

> **_“You may now learn the CLI.”_**

You don’t wait for someone to officially classify you as:

> **_“Cloud person.”_**
> 
> **_“Hardware person.”_**
> 
> **_“AI person.”_**
> 
> **_“Developer.”_**

Technology does not care about your categories.

The problem simply exists.

And the Renaissance Developer asks:

> **_“What do I need to understand to solve this?”_**

The Renaissance Developer Is Not A Person Who Knows Everything
--------------------------------------------------------------

This is important.

A Renaissance Developer is not someone who knows everything.

That person does not exist.

Especially now.

There is simply too much technology.

Too many systems.

Too many abstractions.

Too many rapidly evolving tools.

The Renaissance Developer is someone who can move.

Across disciplines.

Across abstraction layers.

Across environments.

Across problems.

One week you might be writing Python.

The next week you might be learning Kubernetes.

Then experimenting with Agentic AI.

Then reading documentation about an API.

Then debugging a cloud architecture.

Then apparently…

**Performing laptop surgery.**

The goal is not to know everything.

The goal is to never become intellectually trapped.

Timelessness Is Essential
-------------------------

This brings me to something I think is becoming increasingly important.

### Timelessness.

Technology changes.

Tools change.

Frameworks change.

AI models change.

IDE features change.

Companies change.

Platforms disappear.

New abstractions appear.

And the cycle continues.

But some knowledge remains valuable across technological eras.

Understanding how computers work.

Understanding operating systems.

Understanding networks.

Understanding storage.

Understanding programming fundamentals.

Understanding systems.

Understanding architecture.

Understanding how to learn.

These things have longevity.

The tools sitting on top of them may change.

But the underlying concepts continue to matter.

This is why I think timeless knowledge is becoming more important, not less, in the AI era.

Because if everything around you changes quickly, you need something stable to stand on.

### Learn The New Things And Keep The Old Knowledge.

We should absolutely learn AI.

Learn Agentic systems.

Learn cloud platforms.

Learn new programming languages.

Learn new frameworks.

Experiment with new tools.

But we should also maintain an understanding of the fundamentals underneath them.

**_Because eventually, the abstraction may fail._**

The agent may behave unexpectedly.

The application may stop working.

The environment may become corrupted.

The GUI may not solve the problem.

And suddenly…

You need to understand what exists underneath.

So Yes…
-------

### A few months ago, a freshly released Agentic IDE turned me into a Renaissance Developer.

It introduced me to a problem that moved me through multiple layers of computing.

AI.

Software.

Operating systems.

The CLI.

Storage.

Hardware.

Backup.

Recovery.

And eventually…

Back to AI again.

**Because after all of that…**

_After the screwdrivers._

_After removing the drive._

_After putting it into an enclosure._

_After connecting it to another computer._

_After backing up my files._

_After recovering my laptop from scratch._

_After everything…_

**I downloaded the IDE again.**

😂😂😂

Because curiosity did not disappear.

I just had more context now.

And perhaps that is the entire point.

A Renaissance Developer does not stop exploring because technology becomes complicated.

They explore more carefully.

They learn.

They understand.

They recover.

And then…

They build again.

The Renaissance Developer In The Age Of AI
------------------------------------------

AI is not reducing the need for technical knowledge.

In many ways, it is expanding the environments we need to understand.

The future developer may need to move comfortably between:

*   Cloud
*   AI
*   Local Computing
*   CLI
*   Hardware
*   Documentation
*   Security
*   **Systems Thinking**

And perhaps the most important skill connecting all of them:

### The ability to teach yourself what you need to know next.

That is the real Renaissance Developer.

Not someone who knows everything.

Someone who keeps learning.

Someone who can move across disciplines.

Someone who understands that the abstraction layer is useful…

but that reality still exists underneath it.

Someone who is curious enough to download the new tool.

Responsible enough to understand what it is doing.

_And resilient enough to take apart a laptop if necessary._

In Conclusive Other Words…
--------------------------

Be curious.

Learn deeply.

Learn broadly.

Understand the new.

Respect the fundamentals.

Learn the cloud.

Learn AI.

Learn your local machine.

Learn the CLI.

_Maybe learn where the screwdriver is. 😂_

Because in the age of AI…

**Timelessness is essential.**

And perhaps that is what it truly means to be a:

### Renaissance Developer.
