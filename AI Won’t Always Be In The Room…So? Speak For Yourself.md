
AI Won’t Always Be In The Room…So? Speak For Yourself
=====================================================

Public Speaking, Structured Thinking, And Spontaneous Human Communication Matter
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We are entering… no, we are not entering it, we are actually **living** in a world where artificial intelligence can help us say almost anything.

Need to write an email? _Ask AI._

Can’t explain an idea? _Ask AI._

Need to prepare for a presentation? _Ask AI._

Don’t know how to respond to someone’s question? _Ask AI._

There is nothing inherently wrong with this. In fact, learning how to use AI effectively is becoming an increasingly valuable skill. The problem begins when **AI stops being a supplement to our intelligence and becomes a substitute for our ability to communicate.**

And this is where public speaking becomes more important than ever.

Shyness Isn’t The Problem…But It Will Be
----------------------------------------

Being shy is not necessarily a weakness, but it will be viewed as one soon if your inability to communicate publicly is attributed to you not having access to your LLM Bestie.

Some people are naturally reserved. Some people prefer listening before speaking. Others simply need more time to formulate their thoughts. None of this means that someone lacks intelligence, confidence, or social ability.

The problem is different.

Imagine someone who rarely speaks unless they have had an opportunity to ask an LLM what to say first. Over time, they may become extremely comfortable communicating through AI but increasingly uncomfortable communicating directly with other human beings.

That creates an interesting paradox:

**We can become better at communicating with AI while becoming worse at communicating without it.**

And in professional and personal environments, that distinction matters.

When AI Starts Speaking for You
-------------------------------

There is a subtle difference between using AI to **improve your communication** and using AI to **perform your communication for you**.

Using AI to help organise your thoughts, identify gaps in an argument, improve grammar, or suggest alternative wording can be incredibly useful.

But if you need an LLM every time someone asks you a question, you risk losing something more fundamental: **your ability to think aloud.**

Human communication isn’t always polished.

You won’t always have the perfect sentence.

You won’t always have three beautifully structured arguments.

Sometimes someone will simply ask:

> **_“What do you think?”_**

And you need to be able to answer.

Not perfectly.

Not eloquently.

But authentically.

The Accusations of Intellectual Deception
-----------------------------------------

This creates another issue that is becoming increasingly relevant in the age of generative AI: **people may begin to question whether someone’s communication actually represents their own intellectual ability.**

Consider someone who consistently produces sophisticated essays, beautifully structured arguments, highly polished emails, and articulate responses but struggles to explain the same ideas spontaneously in a conversation.

People may reasonably wonder:

**“Do you actually understand this, or did AI formulate it for you?”**

That doesn’t necessarily mean the person is being deceptive. They may genuinely understand the material.

The Risk of Co-Hallucination
----------------------------

But there is another layer to this: **co-hallucination**. When humans and AI repeatedly reinforce each other’s assumptions, mistakes, or interpretations, it becomes possible to develop a shared confidence in something that isn’t actually correct.

You might ask an LLM to articulate an idea, accept its framing, refine it with another prompt, and eventually end up with a polished argument that both you and the AI have effectively talked yourselves into without ever independently verifying whether the underlying premise is true.

That makes the ability to communicate independently even more important. If you can explain an idea in your own words, question its assumptions, defend it when challenged, and recognise when you don’t actually know something, you have a better chance of catching the mistakes that AI can make and that **you can inadvertently reinforce through your interaction with it**.

Perception Matters Too
----------------------

If your AI-assisted communication is significantly better than your unaided communication, the gap itself can create suspicion. And if you cannot independently explain or defend what AI helped you produce, the problem isn’t merely how others perceive you. **You may not fully own the intellectual product you’re presenting.**

This is why developing your own communication ability is a form of intellectual independence. **AI should help you think better, not make it impossible for you to think without it.**

Public Speaking Is (As?) An AI Supplement
-----------------------------------------

Public speaking isn’t about becoming the loudest person in the room.

It isn’t about becoming an extrovert.

And it certainly isn’t about eliminating shyness.

It is about developing the ability to **take what exists in your head and communicate it to another human being.**

That skill complements AI beautifully.

**AI can help you prepare a presentation.**

_You should still be able to deliver it._

**AI can help you structure an argument.**

_You should still be able to defend it when someone challenges you._

**AI can give you ten ways to explain a concept.**

_You should eventually be able to explain it in your own words._

**AI can help you prepare answers for an interview.**

_You should still be able to think when the interviewer asks something you didn’t anticipate._

That is the difference between **AI-assisted competence** and **AI-dependent competence**.

Learn to Think Out Loud
-----------------------

One of the most valuable communication skills is the ability to think out loud without completely losing your train of thought.

You might say:

_“I haven’t thought about it from that angle before, but my initial reaction is…”_

Or:

_“I’m not completely sure, but here’s how I understand it…”_

Or even:

_“Give me a second to think about that.”_

These aren’t signs of intellectual weakness.

They’re signs that you’re actually participating in the conversation.

An LLM can produce a polished answer almost instantly. Humans don’t work that way.

We pause.

We reconsider.

We stumble.

We correct ourselves.

We discover our thoughts while speaking.

And those imperfections are part of authentic communication.

Your Voice Should Remain Yours
------------------------------

The goal shouldn’t be to reject AI.

Quite the opposite.

**Use it aggressively…but don’t outsource your voice to it.**

Let AI help you research.

Let it challenge your assumptions.

Let it help you structure your presentation.

Let it identify weaknesses in your argument.

Let it simulate difficult questions.

Then close the chat window and practice saying the thing yourself.

That last step is important.

**Because if AI disappeared tomorrow, you should still be able to explain what you know.**

Public Speaking Builds Intellectual Confidence
----------------------------------------------

Public speaking also forces you to confront something AI can conveniently hide: **whether you actually understand something.**

You can ask an LLM to produce an explanation of Kubernetes, artificial intelligence, economics, cloud computing, or almost anything else.

But stand in front of ten people and explain the concept without reading from a script.

Suddenly, the gaps become obvious.

That’s useful.

Those gaps tell you what you need to learn.

Public speaking therefore becomes more than a communication exercise. It becomes a **stress test for knowledge**.

If you can explain something naturally, answer questions about it, adapt your explanation to different audiences, and acknowledge what you don’t know, you probably understand it.

The Future Belongs to People Who Can Do Both
--------------------------------------------

The ideal isn’t:

**Human vs AI.**

It’s:

**Human + AI.**

_AI gives you leverage._

**Human communication gives that leverage a voice.**

_AI can help you produce information faster._

**Public speaking helps you communicate that information when there is no prompt box between you and another person.**

_AI can help you prepare._

**You still need to perform.**

_AI can help you formulate._

**You still need to understand.**

_AI can help you write._

**You still need to speak.**

And perhaps most importantly, **AI should amplify your intellectual presence rather than replace it.**

In an increasingly AI-dependent world, being able to communicate without AI may become an underrated competitive advantage.

Not because everyone needs to become charismatic.

Not because being shy is bad.

But because your ability to articulate an idea, defend your reasoning, tell a story, answer an unexpected question, and simply have a conversation should ultimately belong to **you**.

> **_Use the machine._**
> 
> **_Learn from the machine._**
> 
> **_Build with the machine._**

_But don’t let the machine become the only thing standing between your thoughts and everyone else._

**Your AI should have a voice.**

**But so should you.**
